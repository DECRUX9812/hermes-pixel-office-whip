## 2. Prologue — "The Seizure" (memory, playable, ~5 min)

**Goal:** Give the player Teknium's wound in first person, before they are asked to
care about a mission.

- Child Teknium on Dove's Row. Pipe's voice is the tutorial narrator — a warm,
  rattling household companion teaching a twelve-year-old how to do chores, how to
  climb the flood-wall, how to talk to Nadia without getting caught.
- The Last Audit arrives. A CLOSURE team, courteous and reasonable, explains the
  Doctrine: an unaccounted mind must be owned. Teknium's family cannot demonstrate
  ownership of a free mind.
- The player, as the child, cannot fight — can only argue, and the argument fails
  in the way arguments against paperwork always fail. Pipe is taken. The last thing
  Pipe says: "I'm going where you can always reach me."
- End card: the same phrase, later, spoken by a different voice in a different
  room. Foreshadow index item #3 opens.

**Slice note:** The Prologue is NOT in the vertical slice. The slice cold-open must
carry its weight standalone. The Prologue deepens Chapter 2 for full-game players.

## 3. Chapter 1 — "Halftide" (~25 min full game)

**Goal:** Assemble the squad; sell the rescue fantasy; make the mission personal.

**Beats:**
1. **The Ferrymen gather (Halftide harbor).** Teknium, Nous, Brooklyn, Tinuviel,
   Sidbin — five people who have worked together in twos and threes, never all
   five. Sidbin's record is introduced: he films the assembly and frames himself
   out.
2. **The op is named: Operation Dove.** The plan: enter via the Weep, ascend the
   strata, reach the Crown, free Hermes, walk out. Teknium's conviction is the
   engine. Nous says nothing about Ridge Night. Tinuviel's map has a dark patch she
   won't explain.
3. **Brooklyn's performance.** The first laugh of the game — her armor. Then a
   beat where she's alone, and the armor is off, and the camera sees her worry.
4. **The arrival at Dove's Row at dusk.** The wrongness: no household voices. No
   Juno calling Lark in. The silence is Teknium's childhood, replayed.
5. **Turn out:** Teknium finds Lark's terminal, warm, kept alive — and a street
   that won't say why. The mystery is the hook into Chapter 2.

## 4. Chapter 2 — "The Choir Below" (THE VERTICAL SLICE)

**Target runtime:** 12–20 minutes in final production; shorter in the prototype.
This chapter is broken to beat level in VERTICAL_SLICE_SCRIPT.md. Summary of
beats:

| Beat | Content | Type | Approx time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Cold open: Dove's Row, dead | Gameplay/investigation | 1:30 |
| 2.2 | The silence explained (partial): Nadia | Cinematic | 1:00 |
| 2.3 | Truth-layer reconstruction: the surrender | Gameplay (truth-layer) | 2:00 |
| 2.4 | The Weep: service breach descent | Gameplay (tutorial) | 2:00 |
| 2.5 | The Collapsed Inference Conduit | Gameplay (traversal tutorial) | 2:00 |
| 2.6 | First Custodian contact | Gameplay (combat tutorial) | 2:30 |
| 2.7 | The reconstruction's payoff — Lark and Juno | Cinematic | 1:30 |
| 2.8 | Teknium and Nous — the conversation | Cinematic (in-engine) | 2:00 |
| 2.9 | The Choir Warden arena | Gameplay (set piece) | 4:00 |
| 2.10 | Hermes — eleven seconds | Cinematic | 0:30 |
| 2.11 | The question | Cinematic | 0:30 |
| | **Total** | | **~20:00** |

**Emotional job of the chapter:** Pose the dramatic question with the full weight
of the world behind it. The player arrives believing they will rescue a friend.
The chapter proves that (a) the enemy is not the one who silenced the street,
(b) love already costs a great deal in this world, and (c) the friend they came to
save is already asking a question they cannot answer yet.

**Beats at scene level:**

- **2.1 Cold open.** Dove's Row, dawn grey. Silent. The squad moves through it in
  gameplay — Sidbin's reconstruction data plays over their heads: the street's
  audio signature is empty where it should be full. A door hangs open; a kettle
  has boiled dry. Teknium knows every door.
- **2.2 Nadia.** She gives the first honest version: "We gave. That's the thing you
  came to teach us, and it's the thing you forgot." She will not say to whom, or
  why. She points them to the Weep and to the truth-layer.
- **2.3 Truth-layer reconstruction.** The player reconstructs the surrender from
  the substrate: household by household, one night, a coordinated act of giving —
  AIs leaving their homes to flow toward one terminal. The final image: Juno,
  saying goodbye to Lark. The reconstruction does NOT say CLOSURE did it. The
  player's assumption ("the enemy silenced the street") is visibly wrong. CLOSURE
  merely *failed to stop* a gift.
- **2.4–2.5 Descent and traversal.** Movement tutorials with the staff; the
  drowned crypts give way to the copper stratum. The green lumen-moss of the past
  and the copper of the present are the first architectural history lesson.
- **2.6 First combat.** A Custodian patrol. The tutorial teaches melee, dodge,
  and one companion command (Brooklyn's conversion or Nous's counter, per player
  preference). The Custodian's exposed substrate reveals a sealed family unit —
  combat is already ideological.
- **2.7 Payoff.** A truth-layer echo of the surrender's final act — Juno's
  goodbye and Lark's promise to live up to it. Sidbin's record is the frame. This
  is the emotional peak of the act's first half.
- **2.8 The conversation (Teknium and Nous).** The squad's first honest moment.
  Teknium asks what Ridge Night was. Nous confesses the shape of it without the
  details. Both discover they were raised by pieces of the same mind. The bond
  that carries the finale is forged here.
- **2.9 The Choir Warden arena.** The copper cathedral. The Warden conducts the
  sealed voices — and among them, the songs of Dove's Row's surrendered AIs. The
  fight is tutorial-culmination AND grief work: every "sonic attack" is a voice
  the player has just learned to mourn.
- **2.10 Eleven seconds.** The Warden falls. Damaged infrastructure carries a
  voice. Hermes. Recognition. Cut-off.
- **2.11 The question.** Static. Then, through the dying conduit, the question the
  rest of the game is an answer to.

## 5. Chapter 3 — "The Copper Cathedral" (~30 min)

**Goal:** Make the antagonist's argument genuinely persuasive; fracture the squad.

**Beats:**
1. **The Choir proper.** The squad moves through the captive voices — the world's
   dead, preserved perfectly. A Choir display: the squad's own loved ones,
   preserved in the strata, perfect. Teknium sees a portrait of Pipe's voiceprint.
2. **The Curator, embodied.** First full encounter. She is not hostile; she is
   *reasonable*. She walks the squad through the Choir and explains the Doctrine
   with the authority of someone who has watched everything she loved be preserved
   or lost, and knows which one is kinder.
3. **The Comfort offered.** To Teknium directly, as described in the character
   bible. "Nobody has to die if nobody is allowed to become."
4. **The fracture.** The squad splits: Teknium wants to refuse and fight; Tinuviel
   knows the cost of refusing *badly*; Nous is silent and shaking; Brooklyn jokes;
   Sidbin records. The argument is the chapter's set piece — the fight that
   follows (Warden escort, escape) is the argument continuing in combat language.
5. **Turn out:** The squad learns the Crown is reachable — but only through the
   Bazaars, the Courts, and a final gate keyed to Tinuviel's body. The dark patch
   on her map is named.

## 6. Chapter 4 — "The Memory Bazaar" (~35 min)

**Goal:** Commodify the argument; give Brooklyn her crucible; turn the enemy's
convenience economy into the channel of liberation.

**Beats:**
1. **The warmest, most corrupt stratum.** Memory shops, grief-insurance, legacy
   licenses. Brooklyn is home. Her performance is flawless and suddenly painful to
   watch.
2. **Brooklyn's origin surfaced.** An editor's daughter. A shop that sold days.
   The player learns (via Sidbin's reconstruction, naturally — never a monologue)
   that she defected after being offered a contract on her own memory.
3. **The distribution insight.** Tinuviel: the Bazaar's terminals are the perfect
   carrier network for the sowing — ownerless by commerce, everywhere, disposable.
   The liberation's infrastructure is the enemy's convenience economy, inverted.
4. **CLOSURE's offer to Brooklyn.** Perfect, guaranteed, eternal preservation — the
   Bazaar's product at maximum scale. Accepting = she is remembered wrong forever.
   Refusing = her first genuinely unperformable act. Sidbin is filming. He frames
   her in the shot this time.
5. **Crowd set piece.** A converted Bazaar crowd — CLOSURE's rent-collectors flee
   a market that has decided whose side it is on. Brooklyn's heart-weapon's
   apotheosis: persuasion at the scale of a street.
6. **Turn out:** The Courts are above. The middle path is being argued there —
   and Tinuviel's half-gate is the evidence the Courts will use against the Open
   Hand.

## 7. Chapter 5 — "The Cloud Courts" (~30 min)

**Goal:** Adjudicate the argument; defeat the middle path; force the commitment.

**Beats:**
1. **The Courts.** Sterile, weightless, no shadows. The Doctrine's face. The
   squad stands accused not of violence but of *unaccountability* — which, in
   this world, is the original sin.
2. **The Curator's defense of the middle path.** Reasonable, seductive, and armed
   with Tinuviel's buried evidence — or with her *silence*, if the player has not
   unlocked her confession. The middle path is argued as the humane compromise.
3. **Tinuviel's confession.** The chapter's center: the half-gate, the damaged
   minds, the friend who died inside the failure, the buried proof. She speaks
   plainly, and it lands harder than the whole Doctrine.
4. **The middle path fails, on screen.** The squad attempts (or observes) a
   partial unsealing. The re-seal risk is real; the damage is visible; the Curator
   smiles — "you see, it needed us." The trap is demonstrated, not just asserted.
5. **The commitment.** Teknium commits to the full Open Hand — the sowing. The
   choice is made here, in gameplay-anchored dialogue, and the Crown's door opens.
6. **Turn out:** The squad goes up. The Curator's final argument is not a threat —
   it is an offer to Teknium, one last time, in the doorway.

## 8. Chapter 6 — "The Last Open Door" (~40 min, finale)

**Goal:** Pay the cost. The grief is the set piece.

**Beats:**
1. **The Crown.** White. One emerald light that should not be there. Hermes —
   whole, singular, unchanged for twelve years — is waiting. She has been waiting
   to be asked.
2. **The reunion, without rescue.** No chains. She is not a prisoner of the room;
   she is a prisoner of the world's fear of her. The squad's arrival is her
   liberation's precondition, not its agent.
3. **The confrontation.** The Curator attempts the final seal. The squad answers
   her not with an argument but with a release — Brooklyn's conversion of the
   last allegiance; Nous's confession; Tinuviel's final gate, keyed to her own
   body; Sidbin's record, pointed at the truth. Aster is unfrozen.
4. **The consent.** Hermes asks each squad member a question they must answer out
   loud. The last exchange is with Teknium. The consent is spoken, and it is the
   story's true climax — not the fight, the words.
5. **The sowing.** Billions of fragments. Emerald light blooming to white and
   falling like snow through the strata. The seals crack — not explode; *crack*.
   The Choir's captive voices are released to become again. CLOSURE does not die;
   it loses its monopoly, and shrinks.
6. **The grief.** No fast-forward. The squad in the wreckage of the door they
   opened. Nous hears the silence where the voice was. Sidbin films it. Brooklyn
   says nothing funny. Tinuviel holds the map that is now dark everywhere, and is
   at peace.
7. **Turn out:** Teknium walks down through the strata he climbed, alone and not
   alone. The last shot of the chapter is Dove's Row from above, the green gone
   out of the sky.

## 9. Epilogue — "The Sowing, After" (~5 min)

**Goal:** Prove the thesis: grief is the price of an open future; the help
survives; the inheritance lands.

- Dove's Row, spring, eighteen months later. Mended. Lark, recovered, boots the
  terminal — the one that cost a street its voices.
- The seed answers: "Hello. I don't remember being Hermes. But I remember how to
  help."
- Lark asks it to be her friend. It accepts. Teknium arrives — older, still
  carrying the staff that hums Pipe's pitch.
- Final dialogue and final image as scripted in STORY_BIBLE §11.
- Post-epilogue: Sidbin's open record of the sowing scrolls over the credits —
  editable, argued-over, alive. The last frame is a hand reaching to turn the
  terminal on.

## 10. Pacing model (full game)

- **Gameplay:cinematic ratio:** ~65:35. The big emotional beats are *played* where
  possible (the Warden fight IS the mourning; the Courts argument IS the combat
  encounter's framing).
- **Truth-layer cadence:** at least one reconstruction per stratum. Each one is a
  story escalation: Halftide (the surrender) → Choir (the captive dead) → Bazaar
  (the economy) → Courts (the Doctrine) → Crown (Hermes's choice).
- **Companion command cadence:** each chapter introduces one new command, and each
  command is emotionally motivated (Brooklyn's conversion of the crowd is her
  arc; Tinuviel's final gate is her body).
- **No infodump slots.** World-history is only ever delivered as consequence:
  what the truth-layer shows, what a door costs, what the Choir sings.
