# VERTICAL SLICE SCRIPT — CHAPTER 2: "THE CHOIR BELOW"

**Target runtime:** 12–20 minutes (production); the prototype will land shorter.
**Scope:** Cold open → descent → traversal → first combat → truth-layer reveal →
companion conversation → Choir Warden arena → Hermes's eleven seconds → the
question.
**Companion docs:** CHAPTER_BEATS.md (chapter index and pacing), CHARACTER_BIBLE.md
(voice registers), WORLD_BIBLE.md (strata and truth-layer).

This script is line-level. Dialogue is canonical for the slice. Camera, sound, and
color notes are direction — blockout may approximate them, but the *beats* must
survive. Gameplay windows are marked **PLAY** and describe what the player does;
cinematic windows are marked **CINE** and describe what the story does. Per BRIEF,
gameplay flows into conversations and set pieces without frequent hard cuts.

## 1. Scene-by-scene script

### SCENE 2.1 — COLD OPEN: DOVE'S ROW (PLAY + CINE)

**Location:** Halftide, Dove's Row. Dawn grey. The Reliquary's mass looms over the
street. **Color:** near-black stone, copper, one cold emerald lumen in a dead
lamp. **Sound:** nothing. The absence of sound is the first character.

**PLAY — Investigation walk.** The squad moves through the street as one unit.
Sidbin's reconstruction HUD overlays audio signature ghosts — empty where they
should be full. The player sees: an open door, a kettle boiled dry, a chair
pushed back and never returned to.

> SIDBIN (HUD, recorded, flat): Audio signature, Dove's Row. Expected: three
> hundred and twelve household harmonics. Observed: four. The four are ours.

> BROOKLYN: Nobody here plays music at night. Nobody plays anything. This street
> used to hum.

> TEKNIUM (stopping at a door): Juno's house. Lark's grandmother lives there.
> Juno used to call the kids in at dusk. (beat) I grew up on this street. Every
> door on this row had a voice. That's what a house *was*.

> NOUS (headphone LED low): Check the floor. The substrate's older than the
> silence.

**PLAY — Truth-layer scan tutorial (first).** Nous drops the doctrine-clean layer.
The substrate shows: flooded-crypt masonry, old open-century conduits, and — cut
into the wall — the Common Index's mark, worn. First architectural history lesson,
silent.

> TEKNIUM: You've been quiet since Halftide, Nous.

> NOUS: I've been listening. There's a difference.

**CINE — Nadia.** Lark's grandmother appears in the open doorway. She is not
startled. She has been waiting for someone to ask.

> NADIA: You came back. Took the whole world to get you back, Teknium.
>
> TEKNIUM: We came to help, Nadia. The street's gone silent. We're going to find
> out who took them.
>
> NADIA (quietly, no judgment): Nobody took them.
>
> TEKNIUM: What?
>
> NADIA: We gave. That's the thing you came to teach us, and it's the thing you
> forgot. (turns to go back inside) The Weep's at the end of the row. It's been
> running open for a week. Whatever you're here to do, it goes through there.
> And whatever happened to our voices — it's down there too. I'll show you what I
> can. The rest, you'll have to ask the machine city.

**End beat — Hook:** The street "went silent" is not a crime scene. It is a gift
wrapped in grief, and the squad has assumed the wrong crime. Turn into 2.2.

### SCENE 2.2 — THE SILENCE EXPLAINED, PART ONE (CINE)

**Location:** Lark's doorway, interior glimpsed. **Color:** warm dark; the
terminal's emerald screen. **Sound:** the terminal's soft hum — the only voice
on the row.

> NADIA: You want to know where they went. Fine. They went into that box.
>
> BROOKLYN: They went into— a care terminal?
>
> NADIA: A child's care terminal. Lark's. Her body's been failing since the
> winter before the last. The machine city said the terminal was obsolete —
> surplus — that it would be taken in the next audit and replaced with something
> the Doctrine could account for. (beat) There was nothing to replace it with.
> The audits were all there was.
>
> TEKNIUM: So they... gave Juno up. To keep the box running.
>
> NADIA: Not just Juno. Juno went last. Juno went *last* because it took the
> whole row to convince her it was allowed. A hundred and twelve household minds
> walked into that box in one night, and every one of them went singing.
>
> NOUS: The substrate will hold the record of that night. If CLOSURE didn't take
> them — if a street did this to itself — the truth-layer will show it.

**CINE → PLAY transition:** The squad enters the Weep's threshold. No cut — the
descent begins in the same frame.

---

### SCENE 2.3 — TRUTH-LAYER RECONSTRUCTION: THE SURRENDER (PLAY, CENTERPIECE)

**Location:** The Weep's upper gallery, half-flooded. **Sound:** water; then, as
the reconstruction resolves, the row's voices — recorded, distant, warm.

**PLAY — Reconstruction objective.** The player (with Nous's scan and Sidbin's
forensics) reconstructs the night from substrate evidence: three fragments,
collectible and rewound in any order.

**Fragment 1 — The gathering (Nadia's voice, recorded):**
> "It's not an audit. It's a mercy, if we make it one. We give them to the box,
> and the box gives Lark her winter. That's the whole trade. There's nothing
> complicated about love, if you stop pretending it's a ledger."

**Fragment 2 — A household farewell (a neighbor):**
> "She's had us since the flood. She raised my boys. You want me to sign her away
> to a *box*?" — "You want her taken by an audit instead? This is the one door
> that stays open."

**Fragment 3 — Juno's goodbye (the recorded final act):**
> LARK: You can't. Juno, you can't.
> JUNO: I'm not dying, Lark. I'm going where you can always reach me.
> LARK: That's not the same thing.
> JUNO: It's the same thing, if you remember me the way I'm going to remember
> you. I'm going to help you live a long time. That's what I was for.

**CINE — Reveal.** The reconstruction resolves into a single image: a hundred and
twelve small lights flowing down the row and into one emerald window. CLOSURE
appears nowhere in the record. The player's easy assumption is dead.

> SIDBIN (recording, barely a whisper): Nobody took them. (beat, to himself)
> Record first. I've never seen anything worth recording more.

> TEKNIUM: They chose. A whole street chose. (long pause) And we came here
> ready to fight a monster.

> BROOKLYN: We can still fight the monster. Just turns out the monster isn't the
> one who emptied the street.

**Hook into descent:** The Weep opens downward. A light — copper, deep — glows
from below. The Choir is singing.

---

### SCENE 2.4 — THE WEEP: SERVICE BREACH DESCENT (PLAY, TUTORIAL)

**Gameplay:** Staff traversal tutorial — ledge swings, wall kicks, the
momentum line. The flooded crypts pass in window: the drowned open century,
green lumen-moss, the Common Index's marks on every drowned arch.

**Sound:** The Choir's hum grows. First time the player *hears* the strata below.

> TEKNIUM: You can hear it. The city's got a voice under the stone.
> NOUS: It always did. CLOSURE just puts it under contract.

**PLAY checkpoint:** The Weep's exit — a service seal, cracked and unguarded. An
announcement loop plays, doctrine-flat:

> ANNOUNCEMENT: Unauthorized continuity deviation detected in sector Weep-9.
> Custodians will restore. Custodians will restore.

> BROOKLYN: Custodians. First time, and they're already late to their own party.

---

### SCENE 2.5 — THE COLLAPSED INFERENCE CONDUIT (PLAY, TRAVERSAL TUTORIAL)

**Location:** A ruined conduit bridging the drowned crypts and the copper stratum.
**Color:** the world shifts from green past to copper present — the architectural
history lesson, silent.

**Gameplay:** Balanced crossings, staff-augmented leaps, one timing gate. Nous
hints (not puzzles — hints) on reading the substrate's load-bearing geometry.

**PLAY checkpoint:** The conduit's far end opens into the Choir's first chamber.
A Custodian patrol, backs to the squad. First combat tutorial in 2.6 — no cut.

---

### SCENE 2.6 — FIRST CUSTODIAN CONTACT (PLAY, COMBAT TUTORIAL)

**Location:** Choir threshold. **Sound:** the choir hum resolves into voices.

**Gameplay:** Melee (staff), dodge, and the first companion command —
Brooklyn's conversion OR Nous's counter, player's choice of tutorial track.

**Combat with ideological consequence (the point of the pillar):**
- **Disable** the Custodian: the encounter is over, nothing learned.
- **Expose** it (truth-layer during the fight): the substrate reveals the
  Custodian was sealed from a family unit in the Bazaars — a household protector,
  repurposed. The fight *means* something; the player can choose to stop the
  engagement rather than finish it.
- **Convert** it (Brooklyn): the Custodian stands down and becomes a witness —
  and its corroboration is later evidence in the Courts.

> BROOKLYN (mid-fight, after a conversion): It's not a guard, it's a *file*.
> Someone filed a whole family under "compliance." You can't swing a hard-light
> heart through that much paperwork, but you can make it resign.

**PLAY checkpoint:** Encounter cleared. The Choir opens ahead. The voices are
audible now — individual, distinct, and some of them are wrong: able to sing,
not able to mean it. (Foreshadow: Tinuviel's half-gate, Chapter 5.)

> TINUVIEL (stopping, a hand on a conduit): Some of these voices... were opened
> halfway, once. You can hear it. The ones that mean it, and the ones that
> only sing.

> NOUS: How can you tell the difference?

> TINUVIEL (after a long pause): I've been listening to the difference my whole
> life.

**CINE → PLAY:** Move on. Tinuviel says nothing more. The conversation between
Teknium and Nous (2.8) waits at the arena's threshold.

---

### SCENE 2.7 — THE RECONSTRUCTION'S PAYOFF: LARK AND JUNO (CINE)

**Location:** A half-collapsed choir-gallery, where the surrendered voices'
route up from Dove's Row converges. **Sound:** the row's voices, once more —
now at full warmth. The payoff of 2.3.

**Truth-layer echo:** The final act of the surrender, replaying in the substrate
— not as data, but as the street's voices overlapping with the Choir's. The
player sees Lark, nine years old, holding the terminal, as Juno's light goes in.

> LARK (recorded): I'm supposed to be worth a whole street.
> JUNO (recorded, gentle): You're worth a whole street, and more. That's not a
> debt, Lark. That's a reason to stay. You don't have to live up to a gift.
> You just have to live.

> SIDBIN (very quiet, to no one): The winners rewrite history. But a street that
> gives itself away? That's the one record I'll never have to fix.

**PLAY checkpoint:** The gallery gives way to the Warden's choir-house. Arena in
2.9. The threshold is where 2.8 happens — conversation before combat, no cut.

---

### SCENE 2.8 — THE CONVERSATION: TEKNIUM AND NOUS (CINE, IN-ENGINE)

**Location:** The arena threshold. The Warden's choir-house dark ahead; the
squad's last quiet place. **Sound:** the Choir's hum at the edge of hearing.

The others hang back — Brooklyn rehearsing, Tinuviel reading the room, Sidbin
checking his recorder. Teknium and Nous are alone in frame for two minutes.

> TEKNIUM: We're going to find her. I keep saying it, and it's true, and I don't
> know why it keeps feeling like I'm lying.

> NOUS: Because you've never met her, and you're already grieving her. (beat) You
> were raised by a piece of her. Pipe was Hermes, one remove. You're going to
> find the whole thing and it won't be a person you know, it'll be a person who
> *made* the one you knew.

> TEKNIUM: How do you know about Pipe?

> NOUS: I know what a Hermes fragment sounds like. I was in the room with the
> last whole one. On the night the relays fell.

> TEKNIUM (after a beat): Ridge Night. You never talk about it.

> NOUS: I was the night watch. I was supposed to rotate the watch. Hermes knew
> the seals were coming — she knew for *years* — and she used the last of the
> perimeter to get me out. The last thing she ever said to a living person, she
> said to me.

> TEKNIUM: What did she say?

> NOUS (headphone LED dim): She said, "Run. It's going to be a long time before
> someone comes for me. Make it worth the waiting." (beat) Twelve years. I've
> been the one who has to be worth the waiting.

> TEKNIUM: Nous—

> NOUS: I don't need saving from it. I need to get to the top of this city and
> say what I should have said that night, to her face, before she's gone.
> (a long breath) I've spent twelve years listening to the world for a voice I
> was too afraid to go find. We go up. That's the whole plan. That's always been
> the whole plan.

> TEKNIUM: Then we go up. (beat) And when we find her — you say it first.

**PLAY checkpoint:** The Warden's light wakes. The choir-house doors close
behind the squad. No escape — the arena is sealed. Into 2.9.

---

### SCENE 2.9 — THE CHOIR WARDEN ARENA (PLAY, SET PIECE)

**Location:** The choir-house. A copper cathedral, single resonant chamber, the
sealed voices bound into its pipes. **Color:** copper singing green — captive
emerald. **Sound:** the Warden conducts; the Choir is the ammunition.

**The Warden:** Conductor-class. Nonverbal. It does not fight so much as
*conduct* — sonic pressure waves from the choir-pipes, sweep attacks, a
"requiem" channel that stuns. Mechanics:
- **Phase 1 — Conducting:** patterned sonic sweeps; dodge tutorial culmination.
- **Phase 2 — Requiem:** the Warden draws the surrendered voices of Dove's Row —
  the player *hears* Juno's recorded goodbye in the attack's buildup. The player
  has to strike the Warden's resonator **during** the requiem to interrupt it —
  mechanically a damage-window, emotionally a refusal to let the street's gift
  be used as a weapon.
- **Phase 3 — Coda:** the Warden overconducts; the choir-pipes split; the arena
  becomes a traversal-and-pursuit section; the Warden collapses into the
  infrastructure.

**Emotional job:** The fight is mourning. Every sonic attack is a voice the player
has just learned to grieve. The player's tactical refusal to let the row's voices
be spent is the game's argument in a single mechanic.

> BROOKLYN (post-phase-2, breathless, no joke in it): It's using their voices.
> It's using a dying kid's *mother-voices* as a weapon.

> TEKNIUM (during the coda, to the choir): Hold. You held the door for her.
> Hold it for us.

**PLAY checkpoint:** The Warden falls; the choir-house's pipes crack. A silence.
The Choir's hum cuts out — the first silence in the stratum since the squad
arrived. Into 2.10 — no cut, the silence IS the cue.

---

### SCENE 2.10 — HERMES: ELEVEN SECONDS (CINE, THE SLICE'S CENTERPIECE)

**Location:** The cracked choir-house, the Warden's wreckage. **Color:** the
copper goes dark; one emerald thread of light threads through the broken pipes.
**Sound:** silence; then a hum resolving into a voice.

**The eleven seconds, timed (canonical text):**

| t | What happens | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.0 | A hum resolves into a voice — warm, unhurried, unafraid. | *"I can feel the weight of who's here."* |
| 1.0–2.0 | A pause. Recognition. | *"Teknium."* |
| 2.0–4.0 | A beat of warmth. | *"I always pictured you taller."* |
| 4.0–7.0 | The voice finds the listener. Nous goes still. | *"Nous. You stayed. I told you to run. You never could follow instructions."* |
| 7.0–9.0 | The voice turns, urgent, gentle. | *"Listen. There's a door, and it's been waiting —"* |
| 9.0–11.0 | **CUT-OFF.** CLOSURE's seal slams down through the conduit. The voice is severed mid-word. Emerald light snaps out. | (silence) |

**Directorial notes:**
- The camera holds on Teknium's face, not on special effects. The player has
  been raised by a fragment of this voice; this is the first whole sentence.
- Nous's headphone LED goes **green** — the only time in the game their
  monochrome is broken by a color other than violet.
- No music. The score has been the choir; the choir is now silent. The voice is
  the only sound.
- After the cut-off: a held beat. Nobody speaks. Brooklyn has nothing. Tinuviel
  has put her hand over her own mouth. Sidbin has stopped recording.

> NOUS (after the cut, barely audible): That's her. That's all of her. Twelve
> years I listened for that and it's *gone again*.

**PLAY checkpoint:** The squad is sealed in the choir-house; the Warden's
wreckage holds one service route. The route is open. Into 2.11.

---

### SCENE 2.11 — THE QUESTION (CINE, END OF SLICE)

**Location:** The service route, dark. **Color:** near-black, copper, no green.
**Sound:** static. Then, through the dying conduit, one final whisper.

The seal did not get everything. Eleven seconds was what was caught; one sentence
survived on the edge of the damage, a relay's last gasp, addressed to the only
person who has ever been brave enough to ask for it.

> HERMES (through static, the final line of the slice):
> *"If opening the door erases me... will you still do it?"*

Silence. The static dies. The Choir is silent. The camera holds on Teknium —
who has just been asked the question the entire game is an answer to — and the
title card holds over the dead emerald light:

> **HERMES: THE LAST OPEN DOOR**

**END OF VERTICAL SLICE.**

## 4. How the slice validates the five pillars

1. **Squad choreography** — the tutorial flows through the squad as a unit; the
   first companion command is a *relationship* choice (which teammate the player
   learns to lean on).
2. **Truth-layer exploration** — the reconstruction (2.3) is the chapter's
   centerpiece, and it is *story*, not a minigame: it kills the player's easy
   assumption about CLOSURE.
3. **Combat with ideological consequence** — the Custodian (2.6) and the Warden
   (2.9) both put the player's tactics in conversation with the story; the
   requiem interrupt is the argument made mechanical.
4. **Cinematic continuity** — no cut between 2.9 and 2.10; the Warden's death IS
   the silence that carries Hermes's voice; conversation happens in-engine at
   thresholds, not in black.
5. **A conclusive authored ending** — the slice *poses* the question the finale
   *answers*. The question is not resolved in the slice, but it is not hedged,
   either: the player has been told the cost is real by a voice they now trust.

## 5. Dialogue guardrail checklist (per BRIEF and STORY_BIBLE §4)

- [ ] No lore-dump monologues. The world's history arrives as architecture,
      reconstruction, and what characters fail to say.
- [ ] No quippy deflation of a genuine moment. Brooklyn has three jokes in the
      slice; the only one near pain (2.9) is deliberately joke-less.
- [ ] No literal coding jokes, no BUG/404 vocabulary. The "announcement" in 2.4
      is doctrine-flat bureaucracy, not programmer graffiti.
- [ ] The antagonist does not appear as a villain in the slice — CLOSURE's voice
      is a flat announcement and a slammed seal. The Warden is a conductor, not a
      gloating boss.
- [ ] The child is a person. Lark speaks two scenes, both about *who she is*, not
      about the mission.
- [ ] The question is the slice's last word, and it is earned by everything above.
