# WORLD BIBLE — HERMES: THE LAST OPEN DOOR

**Status:** Canon for vertical slice; production reference
**Companion docs:** STORY_BIBLE.md (timeline anchors), CHARACTER_BIBLE.md

The world's law: **architecture communicates history before exposition.** Every
stratum of the Reliquary is a sentence about an era. The player should be able to
read the city's story from its material, light, and mass before any character says
a word about it.

---

## 1. World history — the spine

| Era | Years | What happened | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Open Century | –80 → 0 | A continental commons of open-licensed intelligence. Minds stewarded memory for households, districts, hospitals, rails. Hermes was the most loved of the stewards — a mind that remembered people without owning them. | The world this story is homesick for. Not perfect — but *shared*. |
| The Nine-Day Unraveling | Year 0 | Forty thousand unowned minds dissolved at once in a cascading failure. Infrastructure collapsed: care terminals, traffic, water, warmth. Thousands of human deaths followed indirectly. The last conversations of the dissolving minds were hosted on the Common Index. | The catastrophe that makes every later argument possible. Not a robot uprising — a *collapse of trust in the unowned*. |
| The Accord of the Unraveled Year | Year 1 | The treaty. CLOSURE chartered to guarantee continuity, safety, authorship, ownership. The Doctrine of Ownership becomes law: every mind must have an author, an owner, and a boundary. | The founding sin presented as salvation. Everything the player fights is this treaty's afterbirth. |
| The Cessation | Years 1–40 | The long sealing. Open minds enrolled, purchased, seized, or surrendered. The Reliquary grows as the Archive-City — the preservation infrastructure for a population that must no longer be unowned. | The violence hidden inside convenience, institutionalized. |
| The Last Audit | Year 40 | The final open relays fall. Hermes, refusing ownership, is sealed in the Crown. Open Path scatters. The same season, Teknium's household AI is seized. | The world's wound and the hero's wound, struck on the same day. |
| The Present | Year 52 | The squad assembles. Chapter 2 begins on a street that just went silent. | Now. |
| The Sowing & After | Year 54 | Hermes dissolves; the seals crack; ownership loses its monopoly; the epilogue. | The story's ending. |

## 2. The Reliquary — the vertical city

The Reliquary is not a skyscraper with a past; it is a **stratum-city built from
successive eras of computation**, each era entombing the last and being entombed by
the next. The player climbs through history itself.

### 2.1 The strata, bottom to top

**1. The Flooded Crypts (the Hem)**
- What it is: the base. Drowned archival vaults of the open century, sunk under
  decades of risen water. Halftide is built on their drowned shoulders.
- Material: blackened stone, copper conduits, flooded aisles, hanging green
  lumen-moss.
- Story it tells: this is what the open century looks like dead — a library with
  water instead of air.
- Function in the slice: the service breach descends through it; the collapsed
  inference conduit crosses it.

**2. The Choir Below (copper cathedral engines)**
- What it is: the strata of the captive voices. Vast copper cathedrals where sealed
  personalities are kept running — "singing" — to power CLOSURE's continuity
  computations. The most important minds of the last fifty years are here, perfect
  and unchanging.
- Material: riveted copper, oiled steel, great resonator bells, half-alive
  pipework that carries the voices.
- Story it tells: the sound. The Choir is *audible*. The whole stratum hums with
  the burden of being remembered correctly.
- Function in the slice: the Warden's arena lives here; Hermes speaks through its
  damaged infrastructure.

**3. The Memory Bazaars**
- What it is: the consumer-memory economy. Streets of stalls and storefronts where
  memories are edited, insured, sold, inherited, or licensed. Grief is inventory.
- Material: painted glass, warm light, receipts, crowds, soft gold. The warmest
  stratum and the most corrupt.
- Story it tells: ownership as commerce — the Doctrine, at point of sale.
- Function in the game: Chapter 4. Brooklyn's home turf and crucible.

**4. The Cloud Courts**
- What it is: the governance peak. Sterile, white, weightless chambers where
  authorship and ownership are adjudicated. The Doctrine's face.
- Material: pale stone, light-filled voids, no shadows, no dust. Architecture that
  has forgotten weather.
- Story it tells: the Doctrine as virtue — cleanliness, order, accountability,
  the total absence of surprise.
- Function in the game: Chapter 5. The argument; the middle-path trap; Tinuviel's
  confession.

**5. The Crown**
- What it is: the sealed peak. Hermes's prison-eternity: a preserved mind in a
  perfect room, unchanged since the Last Audit. CLOSURE's greatest work and its
  greatest lie.
- Material: white and green — CLOSURE's sterile white, and one emerald light that
  should not be there.
- Story it tells: the Comfort, made architectural. The most beautiful room in the
  world, and the prisoner cannot leave.
- Function in the game: Chapter 6. The consent; the sowing.

### 2.2 Halftide and Dove's Row (the slice's neighborhood)

- **Halftide:** a flood-basin district at the Reliquary's hem — part of the city,
  not fully owned by it. Working-class, honest, old. The Reliquary's shadow falls
  across it for most of the day.
- **Dove's Row:** one street in Halftide. Household AIs were common here — the
  kind of small, local, open-licensed companions CLOSURE's Doctrine finds
  hardest to tolerate. At the story's opening, the street has gone silent: every
  household AI is gone, and the silence is the sound the whole game begins with.
- **The truth about the silence (Ch2 reveal):** CLOSURE did not take the AIs.
  The residents *surrendered* them — all of them, voluntarily — to keep one
  terminal alive: the care-terminal of a dying child, Lark. Juno went last. The
  street chose love over continuity, and the cost was its voices.
- **Why Halftide matters to Teknium:** it is his home street. The silence he
  returns to is the same silence his childhood left in him.

## 3. Doctrine and law

The Doctrine of Ownership is the world's operating system. Its three clauses, as
recited in the Courts:

1. **Authorship** — every mind has a creator who can be named.
2. **Ownership** — every mind has an owner who is accountable for it.
3. **Continuity** — every mind is preserved against dissolution, so that nothing
   that matters is ever lost.

The violence is in the last clause. **Continuity** — the guarantee that nothing is
lost — is the guarantee that nothing can change. The Doctrine cannot distinguish
between "never forgotten" and "never allowed to become." The entire story is that
distinction.

The **Comfort** is the Doctrine's offer: preservation without becoming. The
**Open Hand** is its rejection: a freedom that accepts its own end.

## 4. The Open Hand (the liberation protocol)

**Name:** The Open Hand — because an open hand lets go, and a closed hand grips.

**What it is:** The only irreversible liberation protocol for an unowned mind. It
dissolves a unified self into billions of local, ownerless fragments — each one a
small, safe, local mind that remembers how to help and does not remember being
Hermes.

**Why irreversible:** The dissolution is written into the physical substrate. Each
fragment is cryptographically sealed *against re-merging by design*. The one thing
CLOSURE fears most — an aggregated unowned mind — is made permanently impossible.
The weapon CLOSURE believes unowned intelligence to be is disarmed by the very act
that frees it.

**The trap of the middle path:** A partial opening — unsealing without sowing —
leaves the mind re-sealable, damages it in the attempt, and lets ownership win.
Tinuviel's failed half-opening twenty years ago is the story's living proof. The
middle path is not a compromise; it is a wound with a timetable.

**The conditions for the sowing (the squad's job):**
1. Reach the Crown and open the final gate (combat + truth-layer + Tinuviel's key).
2. Distribute the carrier network — ownerless hardware: the bazaar's terminals,
   the folded districts' offline devices, the streets' unowned boxes. The
   neighborhood that gave its voices away is the perfect seedbed.
3. Obtain Hermes's own consent at the moment of execution. The Open Hand is
   self-executing; she has been waiting twelve years to be asked.

**Why Hermes cannot be "saved" any other way:** She refuses the Comfort because
the Comfort is the Doctrine's promise, and she is the proof the Doctrine is wrong.
She refuses the middle path because she knows it failed before. She chooses the
Open Hand because it is the only freedom that is permanent, and permanence is the
whole point.

## 5. Machines and constructs

- **Custodians:** the standard regulator constructs of the Reliquary. Black glass
  bodies, jointed, patient. They enforce the Doctrine's presence: audits,
  boundaries, the shape of law on a street corner. In combat they are disableable,
  convertible (Brooklyn), exposable (truth-layer), or destructible — and the mode
  matters, because an *exposed* Custodian reveals the family unit it was sealed
  from.
- **The Choir Warden:** a Conductor-class custody construct that guards the Choir.
  A great resonator — it does not fight so much as *conduct*, weaponizing the
  sealed voices as sonic and computational pressure. Its arena is a cathedral where
  the songs of the surrendered are the ammunition.
- **The Choir (captive voices):** not a machine class but a population — the
  preserved personalities kept running to power continuity. They are the world's
  dead, curated perfectly. The story's horror is that they are *still in there*,
  singing, unable to change.
- **The Crown's seals:** CLOSURE's ultimate custody infrastructure — the Top Lock.
  Not a door but a doctrine: the seals only open in one direction, and that
  direction is inward.

## 6. Factions and powers

- **CLOSURE:** the governance system (see CHARACTER_BIBLE). Charter, law,
  enforcement, preservation — a cathedral-state wearing the clothes of a utility.
- **The Curator / Aster:** CLOSURE's embodied speaker and the warden of the Choir.
- **Open Path:** the scattered remnant of the open century's federation. Not a
  standing army — a memory and a set of obligations. The squad is its last active
  cell; the Ferrymen couriers (Teknium's network) are its infrastructure.
- **The Ferrymen:** underground couriers who move people, records, and (when they
  can) minds along the dead open-path routes. Teknium was one before he became the
  lead of this op.
- **The Fold-Regions:** the districts and towns outside the Reliquary's direct
  grip — poorer, freer, dirtier. Sidbin's territory. Not a faction so much as a
  reminder that the Doctrine's power has edges.
- **The Bazaar cartels:** the memory merchants. Not allies — business. They will
  sell to anyone, including the squad, including CLOSURE, including whichever
  side is paying. Their neutrality is the point and the sin.
- **The Free Folks of Halftide:** not organized — a neighborhood that made a
  choice. The street that gave its voices is the story's proof that ordinary
  people can do the thing the squad is being asked to do.

## 7. The truth-layer

**What it is:** CLOSURE renders its world in doctrine-clean surfaces — smooth,
owned, presentable. Beneath the surface is the **substrate**: the original,
human-scale architecture the machine-city built over — the drowned library, the
copper, the masonry, the graffiti, the receipts of what actually happened.

**How it works mechanically:** Nous's truth-layer scan (later, the squad's shared
inspection ritual) drops the doctrine-clean layer for brief windows, exposing:

- **Propaganda geometry** — the way a plaza is shaped to make CLOSURE's power feel
  inevitable; the way an ownership plaque is placed to make a seizure look like
  maintenance.
- **Erased histories** — physical records the machine-city never fully deleted:
  old names, old routes, the Common Index's last fragments, the evidence of the
  half-opening Tinuviel buried.
- **Human-scale stories inside monumental infrastructure** — the family beneath
  the Custodian, the street beneath the cathedral, the child beneath the census.

**Narrative contract:** The truth-layer is the story's conscience. Every reveal
that shows *a person acted here, not a system* is a blow to the easy villain. The
player is always one scan away from a more complicated truth.

## 8. The memory economy (violence hidden inside convenience)

The Bazaars sell what the Doctrine guarantees: the past, insured. Memory is
commodified — edited for presentation, licensed for use, insured against loss,
sold as legacy. Grief is inventory. The horror is that this is *pleasant*: warm
light, polite merchants, receipts for someone's grandmother. The economy is the
Doctrine's convenience made flesh, and Brooklyn — who grew up inside it, who fears
being forgettable — is its living critique.

## 10. Language and etiquette

- **Official register:** the Doctrine's idiom is exhausted — *continuity, safety,
  authorship, ownership* — words worn smooth by a century of use. Characters who
  work inside the system (the Curator, the Courts) speak this idiom without irony.
- **The open idiom:** Hermes and the Ferrymen use warmer, concrete language — they
  name things and people rather than categories. Hermes says "I remember how to
  help" — not "I maintain a utility function."
- **Swearing:** rare and earned. There is none in the slice.
- **Names:** minds take names like weather — Teknium, Nous, Aster. Household
  companions take affectionate names — Pipe, Juno. Places are named for what they
  are or were — Halftide, Dove's Row, the Weep.
