# EMOTIONAL PACING — HERMES: THE LAST OPEN DOOR

**Status:** Canon for pacing; reconciled with CHAPTER_BEATS.md, VERTICAL_SLICE_SCRIPT.md
**Companion docs:** STORY_BIBLE.md (§12 voice/tone constitution), CHAPTER_BEATS.md
(scene list), PAYOFF_LEDGER.md

**Job of this document:** map the story's emotional temperature scene by scene so
the two failures the story cannot survive never happen — (1) grief that is not
earned (a BURN peak with no runway), and (2) grief that never arrives (a story that
talks about loss and never makes the player feel it).

---

## 1. The temperature scale

A 1–10 scale, mapped to the world's palette. Use it in reviews: "this scene reads
at 8 but should read at 4" is a note, not a feeling.

| Temp | Name | What the player feels | Palette |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ICE** | Dead cold. Silence as a character. The void where a voice was. | near-black stone, no green |
| 2 | **DREAD** | Wrongness. Something is missing and the absence is the clue. | grey-grey, copper undertone |
| 3 | **ALERT** | Attention. Watching, reading, suspicion forming. | copper, damp |
| 4 | **WARMTH** | Connection. Humor with a floor; companionship; competence held gently. | green lumen-moss |
| 5 | **DRIVE** | Momentum. The mission, the climb, the plan working. | copper brightening |
| 6 | **TENSION** | Conflict closing in. Argument, approach, a door that may not open. | white sterile / copper edge |
| 7 | **HEAT** | Argument and fight at full. The set piece. | copper singing |
| 8 | **BURN** | Grief, rage, terror, or vulnerability at peak. The moment that costs. | emerald at maximum |
| 9 | **RUPTURE** | The irreversible. The loss itself, witnessed. | white then green-out |
| 10 | **WHITE** | Aftermath and inheritance. Quiet, open, not resolved — *settled*. | dawn grey turning alive |

**Laws of temperature:**
- **No 9 without a climb.** Every RUPTURE is the top of a staircase built in
  earlier scenes (see §5).
- **Grief gets its time.** A BURN or RUPTURE beat is never quipped past, never
  scored over, never cut away from. STORY_BIBLE §12, law 5.
- **The dramatic question is a COLD peak.** The heaviest line in the game is
  spoken at temperature 1–2. Weight is not volume.
- **Repetition is variation.** Each stratum raises the *intellectual* heat (the
  Doctrine gets harder to dismiss) while the *emotional* temperature keeps
  alternating so the player never goes numb.

## 2. The full-game curve (act level)

```
ACT I        ACT II                 ACT III
10                                    .
 9                                  8       9
 8         .   8 .        .          .       .
 7       . . 7   . 7  .  7 . . 7    7 .     7
 6     6       .  6   6    6 6 .   6   .    6 .
 5    . 5 .     .    5 5 5 . 5     5    5   5  10
 4  .4   4 4   4             4     4        4   4
 3 3             3                    .         3
 2   2               2           2       2
 1 1                    1                    1
   P  1  2  3  4  5  6  E
```

- **Act One (P, Ch1–2):** the descent. Temperature rises through warmth to the
  slice's twin BURNs (the surrender's payoff, the Warden's requiem), then drops
  to the ICE of the question. The player is taught to *feel* loss before the loss
  is offered.
- **Act Two (Ch3–5):** the argument. Intellectual heat climbs each stratum; the
  emotional BURNs are placed on the *player's own people* (Teknium's voiceprint,
  Brooklyn's refusal, Tinuviel's confession) rather than on Hermes — so the
  confrontation with her cost lands on people the player already loves.
- **Act Three (Ch6 + E):** the cost. Tension → consent (BURN) → the sowing
  (RUPTURE) → the grief (BURN, held) → the inheritance (WHITE that is warm but not
  saccharine).

## 11. Breath and rhythm rules

1. **No cut between a BURN and a joke.** Brooklyn's three joke-flavored lines sit
   at DRIVE/ALERT transitions (2.1, 2.3, 2.6), never inside or after a BURN.
2. **The big beats are played, not watched.** The Warden fight IS the mourning;
   the Courts argument IS the encounter's framing; the consent IS the set piece.
   Temperature lives in gameplay, not in cutscenes.
3. **Silence is a temperature.** The 11-second scene has no music and the Choir
   has just gone silent — the score has been the choir, so the silence is the
   loudest sound in the game.
4. **Never sit at 7–8 past its usefulness.** A fight or argument that outlasts its
   emotional job becomes noise. End the scene at the moment the BURN is *known*,
   not when it is exhausted.
5. **The question is a thesis, not a cliffhanger.** Ch2.11 at temperature 1 is
   the story's argument in one line; the game answers it. Do not undercut it with
   a "to be continued" energy.

## 12. What this document is for

Use EMOTIONAL_PACING as a review gate: before any scene is built, its temperature
row is checked against the curve above. If a scene's row drifts more than one step
from its planned temperature, either the scene is wrong or the plan is wrong —
and one of the two gets fixed before the build.
